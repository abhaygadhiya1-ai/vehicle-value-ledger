## What it shows

- **Unconditionally, both sides counted, a 12-month contract costs nothing on average:** -0.45% of the residual across every Eurostat market, -1.15% in the core markets, -1.19% on the UK's long record (negative is a gain), against a one-sided 2.10%, 1.04% and 1.48%. X18's finding, now per contract.
- **The cold state costs more, both sides counted, in every long panel:** cold minus all is 1.01 points of residual in the core markets, 0.93 on the UK's long record and 3.38 across every Eurostat market. But a cold contract is a loss on average only in the broadest panel (2.93%); in the core markets (-0.14%) and the UK (-0.25%) it is about break-even. So X6's rule survives two-sided as a relative premium: price a buy-back contract higher when the level starts cold.
- **The group's own book is too short to say:** 61 overlapping windows after the three-year look-back, cold -2.60%, middle -1.21%, hot -0.45% two-sided. Read the longer panels.

## Limits

- **Overlapping windows:** 12-month windows start every month, so neighbouring windows share most of their move; the effective sample is far smaller than the count (X6 part 1's table).
- **Terciles are cut within each panel,** so 'cold' is relative to that panel's own history.
- **The book's panel starts in 2019-12** (three years of look-back first) and holds one boom.
- **Index prices, retail,** understate what a lessor meets (X7), so these are floors.
- **The state is known at the start,** so a state-dependent price is usable; whether a price that moves with the state errs less out of sample was tested by X6 part 5 at 36 and 48 months, not here.
