# Did Tesla's January 2023 price cut move used Tesla values?

Generated by `analysis/tesla_event.py`.

**Short answer: the data cannot separate the cut from a fall that was already happening.** Used Tesla prices in Washington State were dropping fast for months before 13 January 2023, while the rest of the used-EV market was flat. Any before-and-after number from this event therefore mostly measures the pre-existing slide, not the cut.

## Setup

- **Data:** Washington State title-transfer records, which carry a realised sale price and sale date. 26,825 used electric-vehicle sales between 2022-01 and 2023-12 (9,060 Tesla, 17,765 other EV).
- **Treated:** used Tesla sales. **Control:** used non-Tesla EV sales in the same state and months.
- **Event:** Tesla cut US new-car prices on 13 January 2023, by roughly 6-20% depending on trim ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/01/13/tesla-cuts-prices-in-us-and-europe-to-stoke-sales.html), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2023/01/13/business/tesla-price-cuts/index.html)).
- **Model:** log sale price, with a fixed effect per make, model and model year (326 cells), plus age and mileage. One coefficient per month for Tesla relative to December 2022. Standard errors clustered by make-model-year cell. Fitted on 26,630 sales.

## The repricing started four months before the cut

Read the table as three regimes rather than a trend:

| Period | Tesla vs other used EVs | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Jan-Aug 2022 | about **+13.1%** | A stable premium. Used Teslas held their value well above the rest of the used-EV market. |
| Sep-Dec 2022 | falls to 0% | **The slide starts here, four months before the cut.** By the December reference month the premium has gone. |
| Jan-Mar 2023 | **-5.4%** | The three months after the cut. |
| Apr-Dec 2023 | about **-6.1%**, flat | It stops falling and settles. |

- **The premium fell 13.1 points before the cut and a further 6.1 points after it.** About **68%** of the total repricing had already happened by the time of the January US list-price cut.
- **But not before every Tesla price move.** Tesla cut Model 3 and Model Y prices in China by up to 9% on 24 October 2022 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2022/10/24/investing/tesla-china-price-cuts/index.html)), and offered US buyers $3,750 off December deliveries from 1 December ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/01/tesla-offers-3750-discount-for-model-3-model-y-deliveries-in-december/embed/)), raised to $7,500 on 22 December ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-12-22/tesla-discount-7-500-to-take-delivery-of-model-3-or-model-y)). Most of the pre-cut fall is in November and December, alongside those. Only the start of the slide, in September, comes before any Tesla price action we could find.
- **Naive estimate:** the first three months after the cut sit -5.4% below December 2022. That is the number a careless analysis would report as the effect of the cut, and it is measuring the tail of a slide that began in September 2022.
- **This is a parallel-trends failure, not a small one.** Through the autumn of 2022 the median non-Tesla used EV in this data barely moved ($28,478 in September, $28,944 in December) while the median used Tesla fell from $59,600 to $48,278. The two groups were already diverging before the treatment, so the assumption that makes difference-in-differences causal does not hold, and no number in this table should be presented as the causal effect of the price cut.

## What can honestly be said

1. **Used Tesla values fell hard, and mostly before the US list-price cut.** Between September and December 2022 the median used Tesla in this data went from $59,600 to $48,278 while the median non-Tesla used EV barely moved.
2. **The January cut is not separable from that slide.** Two things make a clean estimate impossible: the steep pre-trend, and the fact that January was the *first of a series* - Tesla cut again in April 2023 and repeatedly after that, so the post period is not a single treatment.
3. **The control group is not clean either.** The whole used-EV market softened through 2023, so non-Tesla EVs are a treated control, which biases the measured gap toward zero.
4. **For the case, the useful finding is the timing, not the size.** Residual value moved months ahead of the headline list-price cut - partly on the manufacturer's own discounts and its cuts in another market, which a list-price feed does not show. A residual-risk model that watches only official list prices is already too late; it has to see discounts, other markets' prices and the used market itself.

## Limits

- Washington State only, electric vehicles only, so this is not a general used-car result.
- Title-transfer prices include private sales and are self-reported; the source contains some clearly wrong values (sale dates written into the price field), so prices outside $2,000-$150,000 are excluded.
- Sales are dated by title transfer, which can lag the handshake by days or weeks. That blurs a sharp event boundary but does not create a months-long pre-trend.
