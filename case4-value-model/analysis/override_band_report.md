## What it shows

- **The engine's calibrated band is far wider than ±5% at every age:** median half-width 23% over all test cars. 74% of adverts lie outside ±5% of the engine's price, so a ±5% band leaves a pricer who knew the true price unable to reach it on most cars.
- **The engine's band does flag its large errors** (unlike part 3's apartment measure): rank correlation 0.35 between width and error over all test cars, 0.14 to 0.41 within markets. The widest fifth has 3.1 times the narrowest fifth's mean error and holds 35% of all the engine's error.
- **Upper bound (a pricer who knows the price):** ±5% lets them remove 29% of the engine's error; the same average latitude tied to the band removes 29%, but on cars 12 years and older leaves 19.0% against 22.0%; the full band removes 85%.
- **A band tied to the calibrated band at the same average width as ±5% is never worse than ±5%** on the grid, at any level of pricer information, under all four readings of the pricer's noise (break-even 0.00). It moves latitude from young cars (median cap 3.4% at 2-4 years) to old ones (8.2% at 12+), where the engine errs most. On cars 12 years and older it is worse than ±5% only at rho² 0.00. Uptake should not suffer: in Dietvorst's data the band's width barely moved it.
- **The full calibrated band (A11's repair as written) needs an informed pricer.** It beats ±5% only once the pricer sees a share of the engine's error of 0.20 to 0.40, and beats the engine alone from 0.20 to 0.30. Even ±5% beats the engine alone only from 0.05 to 0.10. Lay people pricing apartments carried almost none of the model's error (rho² 0.004, part 3); what a pricer who inspects the car carries is unmeasured, and the pilot can measure it.

## Limits

- **The pricer's information is the unknown.** No source measures how much of the engine's error a used-car pricer can see; it is swept. Lay people pricing apartments from a listing saw almost none of the model's error (part 3); a pricer who inspects the car sees condition, damage and options the engine can't.
- **Naive advice weighting is imposed** (one weight on every car). A pricer who weights by their information would do better under every band, most under the widest.
- **Post-hoc capping.** Under a real cap people move less than it allows (Dietvorst), which shrinks both the noise and the signal.
- **Advert prices** and today's market: the calibration guarantee does not cover a moved level (X7).
