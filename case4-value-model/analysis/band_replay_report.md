## What it shows

- **Any band gets the model used; its width barely matters.** Uptake rose from 47% with no adjustment to 68%-71% with a band of 2, 5 or 10 percentiles.
- **People use part of the band, not all of it:** 50%, 52% and 67% of a ±10, ±5 and ±2 band on average. Only 4% of capped model users pressed against the cap on their largest changes, against 62% of free adjusters who went beyond it: a cap scales moves down, it doesn't just trim the big ones.
- **People weight the model the same where it is badly wrong.** On the unusual apartment the median weight on the model was 0.50, as on typical ones (0.50), although 79% of their own first prices were closer to the sale price there, against 26% on typical apartments. This is naive advice weighting on real price estimates.
- **With no private information, the model alone was best on typical apartments** (14.4%) and free adjustment worst (20.6%). Free adjustment was best only on the unusual apartment (81.9% against 91.6% for the model).
- **The tied band did no better than a fixed one of the same average width** (within 0.3 points on all eleven), because the uncertainty measure ranked the unusual apartment 10 of 11: it did not flag where the model was wrong. A band tied to uncertainty is only as good as the uncertainty's ability to flag the model's large errors. Part 4 tests that on the value engine's own band.

## 4. Weight on the model, on real price estimates (experiment 3)

People gave their own price first, then saw the model's. Weight on the model = (final - own) / (model - own), clipped to 0-1, for answers where the two differed.

## Limits

- Capping afterwards is not behaviour under a cap. Under a cap, Dietvorst's people moved less than the cap allowed, so a real cap shrinks moves further than this replay does.
- Eleven apartments, one of them unusual; the pooled column weights them as the experiment did. How often a car is 'unusual' for the engine is set by our own data in part 4, not here.
- Lay US participants who saw only the listing; real pricers see the car. Prices were entered in steps of $0.1m, which is coarse for the cheapest apartments.
- Dietvorst's task gave people nothing the model lacked, so any adjustment could only cost accuracy there.
